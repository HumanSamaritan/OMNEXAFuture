# OMNeXa HR manual screening guide

## Purpose and limits

This guide supports a documented, human-reviewed pre-employment check. It is not a customer KYC programme, a legal determination, or a comprehensive global background-check service. Select checks only where they are relevant to the role, lawful in the person’s jurisdiction, explained to the person, and supported by consent or another valid legal basis.

The HRMS stores the reviewer, check date, source, result and short factual note. Do not enter government ID numbers, upload ID scans, or copy sensitive case records into the general employee profile. Where a government ID is required to verify identity, use the authorised government or licensed provider flow directly and keep its evidence in the approved restricted system.

A possible name match is not a finding. Pause the workflow, compare permitted identifiers, record a concise explanation, and obtain a second human review. Never reject a person automatically based on a fuzzy name match, country risk, protected characteristic, or unverified allegation.

## Official public sanctions and jurisdiction sources

Use the source that applies to the company’s legal obligations and the work being performed. A public list search is not a substitute for legal advice or a complete AML/PEP/adverse-media service.

| Check | Official source |
| --- | --- |
| Singapore / UN-designated persons and entities | [MAS designated individuals and entities](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/anti-money-laundering/targeted-financial-sanctions/lists-of-designated-individuals-and-entities) |
| UN Security Council consolidated list | [UN sanctions search](https://scsanctions.un.org/search/) |
| United States sanctions | [OFAC Sanctions List Search](https://sanctionssearch.ofac.treas.gov/) |
| United Kingdom sanctions | [UK Sanctions List Search](https://search-uk-sanctions-list.service.gov.uk/) |
| European Union financial sanctions | [EU consolidated financial sanctions information](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/eu-solidarity-ukraine/eu-sanctions-against-russia-following-invasion-ukraine/making-sanctions-effective_en) |
| Australia sanctions | [DFAT Consolidated List](https://www.dfat.gov.au/international-relations/security/sanctions/consolidated-list) |
| FATF country-risk statements | [FATF high-risk and monitored jurisdictions](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/topics/high-risk-and-other-monitored-jurisdictions.html) |

FATF lists jurisdictions; they are not lists of individuals. Treat country-risk information as context only, never as a proxy for an individual’s character or suitability.

## Criminal, fraud and conviction checks

There is no single public global criminal-record database suitable for employer name screening. Access and permissible use vary by country and role. Use the official channel for the relevant jurisdiction and follow its applicant-consent process.

- Singapore: the [Singapore Police Force Certificate of Clearance](https://www.police.gov.sg/E-Services/Apply-for-Certificate-of-Clearance) is an applicant-facing service with eligibility rules; it is not a general employer search portal.
- India: the [Digital Police portal](https://digitalpolice.gov.in/) states that crime and criminal data are searchable by authorised police officers. Use the appropriate state or government service where a police clearance is lawfully required; do not attempt to access restricted records.
- Fraud / regulatory checks: use the relevant official regulator, court, or company registry for the jurisdiction and the role. Record the exact source and scope. A registry search is not proof that an allegation is true.

If the official source requires the individual to submit the request, ask the individual to obtain the permitted certificate or result and share it through the approved secure channel. Do not ask for passwords, Singpass credentials, or account access.

## Manual process

1. Confirm role relevance, the person’s location, the applicable legal basis, and consent before starting.
2. Tell the person what categories will be checked, the sources, who will review the result, how long evidence is retained, and how to correct an error.
3. Record consent in OMNeXa HR. The application blocks screening entry until consent is recorded.
4. Search only the official or approved source. Use full name and only the additional identifiers permitted by law and the source’s terms.
5. For every category, record the result, date, reviewer and official source link or reference. Do not paste ID numbers, ID scans, or detailed sensitive records into notes.
6. Mark No potential match only after reviewing the search result. Use Unable to check if a source is unavailable or the identity cannot be resolved. Use Not applicable only with a reason.
7. Route a completed, clear screen to the named HR reviewer. A potential match or incomplete check remains on hold for clarification or second review.
8. The HR reviewer records approve or return-for-follow-up. Approval unlocks the onboarding, document and account-creation flow.
9. Keep the screening evidence only for the approved retention period. Restrict access and follow the company’s privacy notice and local law.

## HR reviewer access

The named reviewer signs in with the approved email and a one-time code. The reviewer screen exposes only the approval queue and the screening record needed for that decision; it does not grant access to payroll or the full employee directory. The reviewer must not approve their own employee record. If the named reviewer is also the employee under review, assign another authorised approver.

## Screening categories configured in OMNeXa HR

The current checklist covers Singapore / UN sanctions, OFAC, UK, EU and Australia sanctions, PEP/adverse-media research, and lawful criminal/fraud/conviction checks. Add another jurisdiction only after HR documents its official source, access requirements, permissible use, consent language, retention rule, and reviewer procedure.

The application does not connect to these sites or scrape their data. HR performs each search manually and records the outcome.
