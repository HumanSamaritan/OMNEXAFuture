# OMNeXa People Operations and HR Records Framework

Version: 1.0 · Owner: People Operations · Review at least annually

> This is an internal operating framework and drafting aid. It is not legal advice or a substitute for a signed contract, current Singapore Ministry of Manpower requirements, or advice from qualified counsel. Confirm jurisdiction, worker classification, actual agreed terms, and current rules before issuing documents or taking employment action.

## 1. Core employee file

Maintain one restricted employee file with a stable employee ID and an audit trail. Collect only information needed for employment administration. Limit access by role and review it periodically.

- Identity and contact details, preferred/legal name, work email, emergency contact where needed.
- Role, manager, department, work location, engagement type, start date, status, and approved changes.
- Signed contract and Key Employment Terms (where applicable), amendments, acknowledgements, and version dates.
- Payroll, leave, attendance, performance, accommodation, and investigation records only where the company has a documented purpose and access control.
- Offboarding checklist, final pay/leave reconciliation, asset return, access revocation, and applicable retention disposition.

Never place NRIC/passport numbers, bank credentials, medical details, disciplinary notes, or compensation in public profiles, visiting cards, analytics events, or unrestricted exports.

## 2. Standard document set

Each issued document should show the legal employer name, employee name and ID, document title, issue date, effective date where relevant, version, confidentiality marking, signatory, and employee acknowledgement where appropriate. Store the generated file and a structured audit entry; record issuer, recipient, timestamp, template version, and acknowledgement status. A generated PDF alone does not prove acceptance.

### Hiring and onboarding

1. Conditional offer / contract of service with the actual agreed terms and conditions.
2. Key Employment Terms schedule: employer and employee identity; job title and duties; commencement and duration; working arrangements; salary period and components; overtime terms where applicable; leave and medical benefits; probation; and notice period. Check the current MOM checklist and statutory applicability before issue.
3. Confidentiality, intellectual property, acceptable-use, information-security, and personal-data acknowledgements, reviewed for enforceability and scope.
4. Onboarding checklist: identity/right-to-work checks as applicable, signed documents, policy receipt, equipment, system access, manager, first-week plan, and training.

### Employment changes

5. Promotion or transfer letter: old/new role, manager, duties, location, effective date, compensation change if any, reporting line, and confirmation of unchanged terms or attached amendment.
6. Compensation change notice: effective date, components, payment period, approval reference, and acknowledgement; restrict access.
7. Performance and development record: goals agreed, check-in dates, evidence, support/actions, review outcome, and employee comments. Apply a consistent review process.
8. Leave and flexible-work decisions: request dates, decision, balance impact, approver, and any agreed return-to-work arrangement. Keep sensitive medical details separate and access-limited.

### Offboarding

9. Resignation receipt / termination notice: legal basis and notice dates, last day, handover, final-pay and leave reconciliation, benefits cessation, and contact point. Use counsel review for involuntary termination or disputes.
10. Exit checklist: property and credential return, account access removal, data transfer, confidentiality reminder, final pay, statutory notifications where applicable, and records retention review.
11. Service certificate: factual role and service dates only, checked against the employee record and approved before signature.

## 3. Document quality and PDF release checklist

- Use a dated, version-controlled template; identify the jurisdiction and worker type.
- Populate terms from approved employee data and an authorised offer record; never infer salary, hours, leave, notice, probation, benefits, or legal classification.
- Validate names, dates, employee ID, amounts/currency, page breaks, signature blocks, and required attachments.
- Escape all user-entered text in HTML and avoid remote resources that can expose employee information.
- Confirm print-to-PDF produces every page, readable fonts, correct page size, no clipped signatures, and no blank/duplicate pages in supported browsers.
- Save the final signed copy in restricted storage; record template version, issuer, issue timestamp, delivery, and signed/accepted status.
- Do not mark a document as issued if audit logging fails; show an explicit error and let an authorised user retry.

## 4. Small-company HR module priorities

### Launch essentials

- People directory and employee lifecycle: hires, status changes, role/manager history, and secure record access.
- Hiring and onboarding: requisition/approval, offer and KET generation, checklist, policy acknowledgements, and access provisioning.
- Leave and attendance: request/approval, balances, public-holiday/calendar rules, time records where required, and exportable audit history.
- Document centre: versioned templates, secure generation/storage, acknowledgements, expiry/reminder dates, and retention/deletion controls.
- Offboarding: resignation/termination workflow, handover, equipment, access revocation, final-pay checklist, exit interview, and service certificate.
- Permissions and audit: least-privilege roles, MFA, access review, activity trail, backup/restore process, and incident reporting.

### Add when processes justify them

- Recruitment pipeline and interview scorecards.
- Performance goals, regular one-to-ones, development plans, training and skills matrix.
- Expenses and approvals, payroll integrations, and benefits administration.
- Workforce planning and aggregate HR reporting with small-group privacy safeguards.

Keep payroll calculations and statutory filings with a validated payroll provider until the company has reliable controls and a designated owner. Avoid adding biometrics, intrusive monitoring, or broad employee analytics without a clear purpose, lawful basis, minimisation, and privacy review.

## 5. Singapore governance checks

- Verify current MOM requirements for Key Employment Terms, itemised payslips, employment records, leave, hours, overtime, and final salary against the worker's coverage and current rules.
- Define retention periods by record category, legal need, and business purpose; restrict access and securely dispose when no longer needed, subject to applicable holds.
- Maintain a personal-data inventory, purposes, access list, correction process, retention schedule, and incident-response contacts under the organisation's PDPA programme.
- Have qualified Singapore counsel review contract, NDA, intellectual-property, disciplinary, termination, internship classification, and cross-border data language before production use.

## 6. Ownership and review

People Operations owns template accuracy and lifecycle steps. A designated approver signs employment terms and material changes. The system owner maintains access controls, backups, auditability, and recovery checks. Review templates and statutory references at least annually and whenever the law or company process changes.
